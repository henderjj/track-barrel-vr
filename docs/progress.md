# Progress and plans

The running record of where the game is and what comes next, so anyone (or any Claude session) can pick up the work. Update it with every feature: what shipped, what the headset check found, and what is next. Feature-by-feature status is in [roadmap.md](roadmap.md).

## Where things stand

- Started from the co-op VR game template: a blocks-and-hoop demo on a wooden deck (removed in F1.1), the start screen and game menu, hands, throwing and catching, the practice crewmate, the lobby, the browser tests and CI.
- The game's concept ([concept.md](concept.md)), implementation plan ([plan.md](plan.md)) and features roadmap ([roadmap.md](roadmap.md)) are written (F0.1, #1).
- Renamed to Track & Barrel VR (F0.2): package and lobby names, the page and start screen titles, and the browser storage keys, now `track-barrel.settings` and `track-barrel.colour`. Settings saved under the old `coop-vr.` keys are not carried over on purpose: on GitHub Pages every game of the account shares one origin, so those belong to the other games there. The lobby Worker is now `track-barrel-lobby`, a new Worker at a new `workers.dev` address on its first deploy; CI builds the game against whichever address it deploys.
- Spike S1, the magnified gunsight (F0.3): a sight (on a post at the deck's far corner, at the gunner's eyepiece since F1.1) looks out at target boards at 100, 250 and 500 m. A second camera renders the view into a 512² texture only while an eye is at the eyepiece, and the lens shows it at infinity at 3× with a reticle and range marks. Practice buttons go to it, aim it at each target and change when it draws, its size and which eyes see it; the perf HUD shows its own cost ([spike note](spikes/s1-sight-render.md)). Passed its headset check on 2026-10-27: 90 fps at 90 Hz at every size, range marks clear. The lens going blank with the eye pressed to the eyepiece (the 5 cm near plane) was fixed by moving the lens back inside the eyepiece; [images/reticle-guide.png](images/reticle-guide.png) shows how to read the marks.
- Spike S4, loading a shell (F0.6, [spikes/s4-loading.md](spikes/s4-loading.md)): a loading station (on the deck behind the start spot, beside the tank since F1.1: ready rack, breech, lever) where a seated player takes a shell, swings it to the breech, rams it and closes the breech, timed and with fumbles counted on a board. Practice → Sit at the breech. Each player's own for now; F1.10 makes loading shared.
- The cabin and seats (F1.1, [cabin.md](cabin.md)): the hull with the driver's seat under a hatch and a vision slit, the turret with its basket, the gun's breech and the gunner's seat, on a wide ground. Each player sits in a seat: eyes at its eye point when VR starts, the head free to move inside the seat's box and stopped at its edge, back in the seat if they stand and walk. The host decides who sits where (the host drives by default) and the guest asks from Practice → Sit in; the crewmate is drawn seated in their seat, and the practice crewmate takes the free seat. The demo, the deck and thumbstick walking are gone. S1's sight now hangs at the gunner's eyepiece, S4's loading station stands beside the tank, and riding (S2) hides the cabin while it shows its own blockout. Every model is now lit by the sky's image-based light (they had been drawn black, as the scene has no lights).
- Seat choice and calibration (F1.2, [seat-choice.md](seat-choice.md)): the start screen's home and crew pages pick the seat, kept for next time. In a crew, seats swap only when both players ask (a sign and the menu's Agree to swap tell the other player); the practice crewmate always agrees. As VR starts, a short calibration takes the eye height while the player holds still (seated by default, or standing) and puts the eyes on the seat's eye point, then records the reach from both hands held out. The menu's eye height (±15 cm from the eye point) replaces the template's character height, and standing up stops the head under the roof.
- Spike S2, riding the tank (F0.4): Test rigs → Ride the tank puts a solo player in the driver's or gunner's seat of a blockout hull while the practice crewmate drives a scripted route and scans the turret, or the player drives and traverses with the thumbsticks. The tank stays at the origin and the repeating ground moves round it; the gunner's seat turns with the turret. Open-top or closed cabin, comfort vignette on or off, all motion within the comfort caps ([spikes/s2-riding.md](spikes/s2-riding.md)). Waiting on its headset check.
- The mechanism kit (F1.3, [mechanisms.md](mechanisms.md)): lever (limits, spring back or notches, detents), crank and handwheel (many turns, stops, ticks, one hand or two on a rim), twist grip with a trigger, two-handled yoke (slower in one hand) and button or switch (fingertip or squeeze), written once in `src/sim/mechanism.ts` and driven by `MechanismSystem`, which takes hold, follows the hands, buzzes and locks the gloves on the handles. A station hands each control to it with `addControl`; its HoldKind's resolver and `holdKindOf` name come with that. Spike S3's tillers are now built from it, and Test rigs → Mechanism kit puts a bench with one of each in front of the player.
- Spike S3, lever feel (F0.5): Test rigs > Lever test puts two tank tillers and a lane board in front of the player. A gripped tiller follows the hand round its pivot, stops at ±25° with a tick and the drawn hand kept on the handle, springs back when let go, and the two drive a tank along a lane on the board. Tracked hands are steadied by a one-euro filter. Written up, with its headset check, in [spikes/s3-lever-feel.md](spikes/s3-lever-feel.md).
- The tank drive (F1.4, [tank-drive.md](tank-drive.md)): the tank moves. The host simulates skid steering, the engine's RPM, low and high gear ranges that grind when shifted with a tiller off centre, the hull following a rolling ground, and soft stops against rocks, walls and houses, all within spike S2's comfort caps (now `src/sim/comfort.ts`). The tank stays at the origin and the ground moves round it. Whoever sits in the driver's seat drives, with the thumbsticks as tillers (W A S D on a PC page) and the gear lever in the menu's new Driving section until F1.5; a guest's controls go to the host and the host's tank state comes back, 45 times a second each. The comfort vignette darkens with the tank's motion. Practice → Drive the tank, and Back to start. The ground is a blockout that repeats every 800 m with a flat start field and a straight lane; S1's targets stand on the lane and show only while the tank is parked at its start.
- The turret and gun laying (F1.8, [turret.md](turret.md)): the turret turns and the gun elevates. The gunner lays it with a power traverse twist grip (6°/s at idle up to 24°/s at top RPM, within spike S2's caps), a manual traverse crank (2° a turn) and an elevation handwheel (3° a turn, −10° to +20°), built from the mechanism kit and mounted in the turret. The gun is its own mesh on trunnions, so the breech dips beside the gunner as it rises. The gunner's page owns the turret and sends it 45 times a second (packet 7); the other page draws it, and the gunner's controls, a little in the past. A seated gunner turns with the basket, and the vignette counts the turret's turn. S1's sight now turns with the turret. Practice → Lay the gun (Gunner, Forward). The tank's jitter buffer became a shared `StateBuffer`.
- The gunsight (F1.9, [gunsight.md](gunsight.md)): spike S1's renderer moved onto the gun as an articulated sight, as on the Tiger: the objective and the sight camera ride on the gun beside the bore, so the view follows the gun, while the eyepiece stays fixed in the turret where the seated gunner leans in, so the eye never chases it as the gun elevates. The gun is left out of its own sight's picture. Sights → Gunsight → Go to it, and Lay on → Ahead, 100 m, 250 m or 500 m lays the gun on that target's range mark from wherever the tank stands (on the range it is driving up), with a line saying which mark to read; from the driver's page it asks the gunner's (event `sight-lay`). S1's stand-in sight and Aim button are gone.

- The test range (F1.7, [range.md](range.md)): the ground is now a gunnery range. A dirt road runs up the drive's lane and another crosses it through the start, both on flat corridors of the height field; three static targets stand at 100, 250 and 500 m (a tank silhouette, an anti-tank gun, a tank), each inside the gunsight's field so the gunner sees all three at once and can lay the reticle's marks on them; a marker post stands every 100 m out to 600 m. Fog (450 to 780 m since John's play test, the sky's horizon colour; it began at 150 m and hid the far target) hides the drawn ground's edge and leaves every target clear; Settings → Range fog turns it off at once (`?fog=0` at load). Practice → Test range → Up the range parks the tank 120 m short of the middle target, facing it. The range is built into the ground's model, so it moves with the world as the tank drives: spike S1's hull-space target boards, which rode along with the tank, are gone, and `src/sim/sight.ts` no longer owns any target numbers. Nothing new goes over the network.
- Loading and firing (F1.10, [loading-firing.md](loading-firing.md)): the gun fires. Spike S4's loading drill moved into the turret: a ready rack of four shells on the wall right of the gunner, the breech (moved 85 cm forward, so its mouth is 50 cm ahead of the gunner's eyes) with its lever on its right side, a casing bin under it and the loading board above the rack. The breech rises and dips with the gun, and a shell in it with it. The power grip's trigger fires a loaded gun: recoil, a muzzle flash, the breech opening by itself, the casing thrown into the bin and the rack refilled. The shell flies at 600 m/s under gravity (`src/sim/ballistics.ts`) with a tracer, and bursts on the ground or a target; the board says what it hit or how far it missed. The gunner's page owns the loading and sends it 45 times a second (packet 8); a shot goes to the other page as a reliable event and the host decides the hit. Practice → Load and fire replaces Sit at the breech.

- The driver's station (F1.5, [driver-station.md](driver-station.md)): the driver drives with their hands. Two tillers (S3's, on pedestals outside the knees) drive the tracks, a gear lever on the gearbox's hump has low (back), neutral and high (forwards) notches and grinds with a rough buzz when shifted with a tiller pushed, and a hand throttle on the left wall raises the idle and stays put. Five gauges under the slit read speed, RPM, coolant, oil pressure and fuel (coolant and fuel move slowly; nothing fails in Phase 1). The vision slit has a steel frame and a brow pad to lean into. The thumbsticks still drive while no tiller is held, and the menu's gear row moves the real lever. The other page sees the driver's levers move: the tank-state packet now carries the gauges and the controls the host drove by.
- The practice crewmate in both seats (F1.13, [crewmate.md](crewmate.md)): one person can test either seat with the crewmate's page on a PC. As driver it takes the tillers and follows the gunner's calls (Advance, Halt, Reverse, Left, Right) or drives a route up the range with three firing halts and back in high gear, changing gear with the tillers centred. As gunner it loads (shell, ram, lever), lays on the nearest range target it hasn't hit with the power grip and the elevation wheel, fires from a halt and calls "On the way!" and "Target!", or "Driver, halt" when the tank keeps moving. Its hands are scripted hands the mechanism kit and the loading take like real ones, so the player sees them on the controls. The game menu has a Calls section for a crew (the gunner's calls to the driver, the driver's Fire at will and Hold fire); a call shows on the other player's sign, and the crewmate's status shows in the menu.
- The periscope (F1.6, [periscope.md](periscope.md)): the driver has a periscope left of the vision slit. Its eyepiece is fixed at eye height a lean ahead; its head on the roof and a two-handled yoke under the eyepiece turn together all the way round, quickly in both hands and slower in one, so the view swings while the eye stays at the window, as on a panoramic sight. Leaning in shows the view at 2× (1× and 3× in Practice) over a wide, short field, rendered only while an eye is at the window by the gunsight's lens and renderer, now shared in `src/systems/scope-lens.ts`. The driver's page owns it and sends its angle (packet 9), so the gunner sees the yoke and the head turn with the driver's hands on it (HoldKind 8). Sights → Periscope: Go to it, Ahead, Power.

- The range run (F1.11, [range-run.md](range-run.md)): Practice → Range run → Start puts the tank back at the start, turns the turret forward and refills the rack, counts down 3, 2, 1, Go! on a sign, and times the crew until each of the three targets has been hit once (concept §14's "how long does a pair take to hit three targets?"). A run clock in each seat (beside the driver's slit, over the gunner's loading board) shows the time, the targets and the shots; a red flag goes up beside each target as it is hit; the third hit stops the clock on the crew's time, kept as the session's best. Stop and Restart from the menu. Practice → Ready to fire (100, 250, 500 m) parks at the start with the gun laid and loaded, so one trigger pull checks a hit; a run that uses it is marked assisted. The host keeps the run and sends it as a reliable event; the clock stands still while the crewmate is away.
- Tank audio and the intercom (F1.12, [audio.md](audio.md)): the tank is heard. A synthesised V12 note follows the RPM (80 Hz at idle to 260 Hz flat out, loping at idle), the tracks rumble and their links clatter with speed, the power traverse whines, and short sounds rendered once at load mark a gear going in, the grind, a bump, a shell rammed, the breech closing or opened by hand, the gun (with the breech flying open) and the casing bouncing in the bin. All Web Audio, no sample files. Each page plays the tank as it draws it, so both crew hear the same tank with nothing new sent. The crewmate's voice now plays plain by default, like an intercom, and the tank's sounds duck 6 dB while either player speaks. Settings → Tank sounds (On, Quiet, Off); Practice → Tank sounds → Play each. The practice crewmate's page stays silent. After the first headset listen (the engine sounded like an 8-bit racing car), the engine became a deep, lumpy V12 diesel beat of 30 to 62 firings a second, muffled by the hull.
- Comfort and play-test stats (F1.14, [comfort-stats.md](comfort-stats.md)): the game menu's new Comfort and stats section sets the vignette's strength (Off, Light, Normal, Strong; it was an on/off row under Driving) and Reduced bumps, where the hull takes 40% of the ground's tilt and eases into it more slowly; the tank rides reduced if either player asks (`comfort` event). I feel queasy marks the moment. Each page counts its own player during a range run (seconds in each seat, talking, fumbles per control, grinds) and writes a row when the run ends, with the crewmate's numbers from their page (`run-stats` event); the log is kept in the browser and saved as a CSV from the menu or the perf overlay's Stats link. That answers the concept's Phase 1 play-test questions with numbers.

- The gearbox (F1.16, [gearbox.md](gearbox.md)), from John's play-test notes (2026-10-30: the two ranges weren't useful, shifting braked the tank, and he wanted an automatic box for easy play, a clutch and gears on the sticks and triggers, a reverse gear and the right trigger as the accelerator): the Sherman's five speeds and reverse, from its ratios with 5th at 35 km/h, the revs following the tracks, and gear changes that coast. Menu → Tank → Gearbox picks Automatic (the default: the tillers drive as before and the box changes up and down itself), Stick shift or Clutch. The last two work a Sherman's controls: the right trigger is the accelerator, the tillers only brake (pull one back to turn on a radius, both to stop), the right stick flicked with the hands on the tillers changes gear (A and B on the sticks), R goes in only when stopped; Clutch adds the clutch on the left trigger, grinding gears and a bogging engine. The lever has seven notches. It takes in F4.3 and F5.2; the engine start-up (F1.17) and a parking brake (F1.18) come next.
- Engine start-up and stalls (F1.17, [engine.md](engine.md)), brought forward from training (F4.4) at John's ask: the tank is found with its engine stopped and cold, and a switch strip on the instrument box starts it: a voltmeter, warning lamps and Battery, Ignition, Choke, Starter and Siren buttons, pressed by a fingertip or held in by a fist so the other hand stays on a tiller. Menu → Tank → Driving → Engine start picks Running (F1.16's tank), Starter button (the default: hold the starter) or Full procedure (battery, ignition, choke, hand throttle, starter; a cold engine needs the choke eased in and a fast idle until warm, or it coughs, floods or stalls). Dropping the clutch in too high a gear stalls it, and it restarts on the move with the clutch in. Without the engine there is no power traverse. New sounds for the starter, catching, coughs, stalls and the siren; the stats count stalls; the practice crewmate starts it.
- The parking brake (F1.18, [parking-brake.md](parking-brake.md)), the last of John's driver's-controls notes: a ratchet lever on a post by the left tiller, pulled up to put it on (it clicks and stays, and a BRAKE lamp lights) and pushed down with the trigger held to let it off. The mechanism kit's lever gained a ratchet.
